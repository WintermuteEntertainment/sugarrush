# Narrative Event Pool

Events are organized by **type** and **difficulty tier**. Each day pulls from these pools at set time slots. Day 1 is fully scripted (see day1-walkthrough.md). Days 2-7 draw from these pools with increasing difficulty.

Events are tagged:
- **[MEAL]** — Happens at a meal slot, involves food choices + carb counting + dosing
- **[SOCIAL]** — Interpersonal interaction, builds confidence
- **[EMOTIONAL]** — Internal/feelings moment
- **[PRACTICAL]** — A logistics/planning challenge
- **[TEACH]** — "Teach a Friend" moment — player explains T1D to someone

---

## MEAL EVENTS

### Tier 1 (Days 2-3) — Guided, Simple Choices

**M1: Pancake Morning**
Breakfast. Someone made pancakes. Syrup is pure sugar. How much syrup? How many pancakes? Companion helps with carb estimation. Teaches: syrup/toppings add carbs fast.

**M2: Sandwich Builder**
Lunch. Build your own sandwich from ingredients. Each item shows carb count. Bread is the big one. Teaches: bread is a primary carb source, fillings vary.

**M3: Taco Night**
Dinner. Family taco night. Shells vs. bowls. Toppings. Teaches: you can make almost any meal work — it's about knowing what's in it.

**M4: Soup and Bread**
Lunch. Soup has hidden carbs (potatoes, noodles, beans). Bread on the side. Teaches: liquids can have carbs too.

**M5: Fruit Salad Surprise**
Snack upgraded to a mini-meal. Fruit has natural sugars. "But it's healthy!" — yes, AND it has carbs. Teaches: healthy ≠ zero carbs.

### Tier 2 (Days 3-5) — Less Guidance, Social Pressure

**M6: Birthday Party Pizza**
Lunch/Dinner. A friend's birthday party. Pizza, cake, soda. Everyone is eating freely. Social pressure to "just eat normal." Multiple choice:
- Count carbs carefully and dose (might feel awkward)
- Eat freely and guess the dose (risky but socially easy)
- Eat moderately and dose for what you know (balanced)
Teaches: social eating is hard, all approaches have trade-offs.

**M7: The School Cafeteria Wildcard**
Lunch. The planned menu changed. The option you researched isn't available. Must adapt on the fly. Teaches: flexibility and estimation when plans fall apart.

**M8: Grandma's Cooking**
Dinner. Grandma made a big meal. She's offended if you don't eat enough. She also insists she knows about diabetes because of a magazine article. Dialogue options for navigating family dynamics while still managing carbs. Teaches: advocating for yourself with family.

**M9: The Protein Plate**
Dinner. A meal that's mostly protein and fat (steak, salad, roasted veggies). Very few carbs. Teaches: not every meal is carb-heavy, and that's fine — you might need less insulin (or none for the meal).

**M10: Smoothie Trap**
Snack. A friend shares a smoothie. Looks healthy. Is absolutely packed with sugar (fruit juice, banana, honey). Teaches: drinks can be sneaky carb bombs.

### Tier 3 (Days 5-7) — Estimation, Unknown Foods, High Complexity

**M11: Restaurant Without Nutrition Info**
Dinner. Family goes to a local restaurant. No carb counts anywhere. Player must estimate from descriptions and portion sizes. Companion offers rough guidance but not exact numbers. Teaches: real-world estimation, portion awareness.

**M12: Cultural Feast**
Dinner. A cultural celebration meal with dishes the player may not have encountered (dim sum, tamales, biryani, injera, etc.). Varied and unfamiliar carb counts. Teaches: all cuisines are manageable, asking questions is okay.

**M13: Cooking Your Own Meal**
Dinner. Player gets to cook/choose ingredients. Must add up carbs from individual components. A recipe card is provided with measurements. Teaches: cooking gives you the most control.

**M14: The Buffet**
Lunch/Dinner. An all-you-can-eat situation. The temptation to overload. Portion control challenge. Teaches: you can enjoy a buffet, but portion awareness matters more than ever.

**M15: Eating When You Don't Want To**
Any meal. Player isn't hungry (maybe feeling unwell, or stressed). But BG is low-ish and they need to eat. Teaches: sometimes eating is medicine, not appetite.

## SOCIAL EVENTS

### Tier 1 (Days 2-3) — Supportive, Simple

**S1: The Curious Classmate**
A classmate notices your device/routine and asks about it. Genuine curiosity, no malice. A "Teach a Friend" moment. Dialogue options range from detailed explanation to casual brush-off. All valid. Confidence +3-5.

**S2: The Supportive Coach**
A coach/teacher pulls you aside: "I read about your T1D. What do I need to know?" Player gets to explain their needs (snack breaks, low treatment, etc.). Empowering moment. Confidence +5.

**S3: The Buddy System**
A friend offers to learn how to help if you have a low. Player can teach them the 15-15 rule. Sweet bonding moment. [TEACH] Confidence +5.

**S4: Show and Tell**
(Younger mode especially) Opportunity to show your class/group your CGM/pump/kit. Can choose to participate or not. Both options respected. Confidence +3.

### Tier 2 (Days 3-5) — Navigating Complexity

**S5: "My Grandma Cured Her Diabetes With Cinnamon"**
A well-meaning person gives unsolicited (wrong) advice. Player must navigate being polite while knowing the advice is incorrect. Dialogue options:
- Politely explain T1 vs T2 differences
- Smile and nod, change subject
- "Thanks, I'll ask my doctor about that" (diplomatic deflection)
Teaches: there is a LOT of misinformation. You don't have to correct everyone, but knowing the truth matters. Confidence +3.

**S6: The Sleepover Dilemma**
Invited to a sleepover. Need to pack supplies, manage meals at someone else's house, decide whether to tell the friend's parents, handle a nighttime BG check in a shared room. Multiple decision points across the evening. Confidence +5 for navigating it.

**S7: Substitute Teacher Doesn't Know**
A substitute teacher doesn't know about your needs. You try to eat a snack during class and get told to put it away. Do you:
- Explain calmly
- Show your medical ID/note
- Ask to go to the nurse
- Comply and feel frustrated (leads to emotional event)
Teaches: advocating in uncomfortable situations. Confidence +3-5.

**S8: Left Out**
Friends are going to an activity that feels hard to manage (amusement park, long hike, pool day) and you're anxious about going. Dialogue explores the feeling, companion encourages problem-solving. Player can choose to go (with preparation) or skip (also valid). Confidence +3 either way.

**S9: "Are You Allowed to Eat That?"**
Someone polices your food. Could be a peer, a relative, a cafeteria worker. Annoying and wrong. Dialogue options for responding with varying levels of directness. Teaches: people will say this. A lot. Having a go-to response helps. Confidence +5.

### Tier 3 (Days 5-7) — High-Stakes Social

**S10: The New Kid**
Another student has just been diagnosed with T1D. They're scared and confused. Player gets to be the mentor. Multiple [TEACH] moments. Very empowering. Confidence +10.

**S11: Team Sport Pressure**
In the middle of a game/competition, you feel low. Coach needs you in. Teammates are counting on you. Do you play through it (dangerous), call a timeout, or sub out? Teaches: health comes first, always. No exceptions. The game should strongly reward the safe choice. Confidence +5 for choosing health.

**S12: The Date / The Crush** (older players in "I've Got This" mode)
A social outing with someone you want to impress. Do you hide your management? Do it openly? What if your CGM alarms? What if you need to treat a low? Teaches: T1D is nothing to hide, and the right people won't care. Confidence +5.

**S13: Social Media Moment**
Someone takes a photo of your pump/injection and posts it. How do you feel? What do you do? Dialogue explores digital boundaries and self-advocacy. Confidence +3-5.

**S14: The Skeptic**
Someone doesn't believe you're "really sick" because you look fine. "But you don't look diabetic." Dialogue options for responding. Teaches: invisible illness is real, you don't owe anyone proof. Confidence +5.

## EMOTIONAL EVENTS

### Tier 1 (Days 2-3)

**E1: The Frustration**
After a BG that went out of range despite doing everything right: "This isn't fair." Companion validates the feeling. No fix offered — just acknowledgment. "Yeah. It isn't fair. And it's okay to be mad about it."

**E2: The Small Win**
Player checks BG and it's perfectly in range after a tricky meal. Moment of pride. Companion celebrates. Confidence +5.

### Tier 2 (Days 4-5)

**E3: Missing Normal**
A quiet moment where the player thinks about life before diagnosis. "I used to just... eat." Companion sits with the feeling. Optionally, a positive reframe — but only if the player chooses it. Not forced positivity.

**E4: The Bad Day**
BG was all over the place. Nothing seemed to work. End of day reflection. Companion: "Some days are like that. Even people who've had T1D for 20 years have bad days. This doesn't mean you're failing."

### Tier 3 (Days 6-7)

**E5: Burnout**
The player is tired of checking, counting, dosing. Everything. "Can't I just skip one?" Dialogue options:
- Vent to companion (cathartic, no BG consequence)
- Skip a check (BG goes unchecked, potential consequence)
- Talk to a family member (support, confidence boost)
Teaches: burnout is real, normal, and manageable — but skipping management has consequences.

**E6: Pride**
End of a tough day managed well. Companion says little. The numbers speak. Player has a moment of genuine accomplishment. Confidence +10.

## PRACTICAL EVENTS

### Tier 1 (Days 2-3)

**P1: Pack Your Bag**
Going somewhere. Need to pack: insulin, snacks, meter/CGM receiver, glucose tabs, medical ID. Forgetting something has a later consequence (but gentle on early days).

**P2: The Fridge Check**
Running low on supplies at home. Need to make sure insulin is stored properly (not frozen, not expired). Quick interactive check.

### Tier 2 (Days 4-5)

**P3: Pharmacy Run**
Need to pick up supplies. A list-management task. Teaches: staying on top of supplies is part of the routine.

**P4: The Site Change**
(Pump users) Time to change your pump site. Where on the body? Rotate sites to avoid scar tissue. 
(Injection users) Rotate injection sites. Same principle, different execution.
Both: a brief interactive body-map where you pick a spot.

**P5: Dead Battery / Lost Meter**
CGM battery is low / meter is missing. Practical problem-solving. CGM users might need a backup finger prick. Finger prick users need to find/borrow supplies. Teaches: always have a backup plan.

### Tier 3 (Days 6-7)

**P6: Travel Prep**
Going on a trip. Time zones, keeping insulin cool, airport security, carrying enough supplies for the whole trip plus extra. Multiple planning decisions.

**P7: Doctor's Appointment**
Visit the endo/diabetes clinic. Review your week's numbers. Practice talking to your doctor. Companion: "Your doctors are on your team. Don't be afraid to tell them what's hard." 

**P8: Emergency Card**
Create/review your emergency info card. Who to call, what to do if you're unconscious, where your glucagon is. Serious but necessary. Teaches: preparation is empowerment.

## EVENT DESIGN PRINCIPLES

1. **No wrong answers in dialogue.** Every choice is valid. Some lead to better BG outcomes, some to better confidence, some to both. None should feel punishing.

2. **Every meal is a learning moment.** Food choices teach real nutritional knowledge — actual carb counts, actual portion sizes, actual foods kids eat.

3. **Social events rehearse real conversations.** Kids WILL be asked these questions. The game lets them practice responses in a safe space.

4. **Emotional events normalize feelings.** Frustration, burnout, sadness, anger — all normal. The game never says "don't feel that way." It says "that makes sense."

5. **Practical events build independence.** Packing your own bag, managing supplies, talking to doctors — these are life skills that build real confidence.

6. **[TEACH] moments are the most powerful.** When the player explains T1D to someone else, THEY are the expert. This inverts the power dynamic from "sick kid" to "knowledgeable person." Use these moments generously.

7. **Representation in NPCs.** Friends, family, teachers, coaches should be diverse in ethnicity, gender, body type, family structure. T1D affects everyone.

8. **Meals should feature real, culturally diverse foods.** Pizza AND biryani AND tamales AND sushi AND injera. Kids should see their own foods in the game.

9. **All nutritional information follows Canada's Food Guide** and Health Canada standards. Carb counts, portion guidance, the plate model (half veggies/fruits, quarter protein, quarter whole grains), and label formatting all use Canadian standards.
