# Scripted Anchor Moments

While most daily events draw from the narrative event pool, specific moments across the week are **scripted** — they always happen, always in the same slot, and form the emotional/educational spine of the week. This gives the week a narrative arc while keeping everything around the anchors randomized for replayability.

## Rhythm: Scripted Days vs. Pool Days

- **Days 1, 3, 5, 7** — Have scripted anchor events (story beats that always happen)
- **Days 2, 4, 6** — Draw entirely from event pools (high replayability)

This alternating rhythm means every replay feels ~50% different while the key learning moments always land.

## Scripted Anchors

### Day 1: Full Tutorial (entirely scripted)
See day1-walkthrough.md. Every beat is fixed.

### Day 3: "The Estimation Challenge"
**Anchor slot: Dinner**
First meal with NO guidance from the companion. Player must estimate carbs in a home-cooked meal entirely on their own. Companion watches silently, gives feedback AFTER. This is the "training wheels come off" moment.

**Why it's anchored:** The shift from guided to independent is a critical emotional beat. It needs to land on Day 3 — early enough to feel challenging, late enough that the player has the tools.

### Day 5: "The Curveball Day"
**Anchor slot: Morning (wake-up)**
Player wakes up feeling unwell. BG is running high for no obvious reason. This triggers the illness storyline — the first major deviation from routine. Sick Day Protocol mini-game replaces the normal morning mini-game.

**Secondary anchor: Evening**
After managing the illness all day, an emotional check-in. The companion sits with the player. "Tough day. You handled it." This is where emotional resilience gets modeled.

**Why it's anchored:** Illness is the most important curveball to teach, and it needs narrative setup (waking up sick) that can't be random.

### Day 7: "Boss Day"
**Anchor slot: Full day structure**
Day 7 has a loose narrative frame — a special event (field trip, family outing, tournament, or similar) that strings the whole day together. Within that frame, curveballs are pulled from the hardest tier of the pool. The day FEELS different because it has a throughline.

**Anchor beats within Day 7:**
- Morning: Setup and preparation (packing, planning ahead)
- Lunch: An unfamiliar food situation
- Afternoon: Physical activity + social pressure stack
- Evening: Reflection on the whole week (companion's most meaningful dialogue)

**Why it's anchored:** The final day should feel like a culmination, not just "another random day." The narrative frame gives it weight.

## Scripted Meal Beats

In addition to the day-level anchors, specific MEALS are scripted to ensure key food-education moments land every week:

| Meal | Day | What's Taught |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 Breakfast | 1 | Carb counting basics (tutorial) |
| Day 1 Dinner | 1 | Estimating without labels (guided) |
| Day 3 Dinner | 3 | Estimating without labels (solo, anchor) |
| Day 5 Lunch | 5 | Eating when you don't want to (illness) |
| Day 7 Lunch | 7 | Unfamiliar food in an unfamiliar setting |

All other meals draw from the pool.

## Replayability Notes

- Scripted anchors are the SAME story beat each replay, but dialogue can vary slightly (companion lines rotate from a pool within the scripted moment)
- The pool events around the anchors change every replay
- Second+ week replays can introduce VARIANT anchors — same story beat, different setting (e.g., Day 3 estimation challenge is at a restaurant instead of home, Day 5 illness is a stomach bug instead of a cold)
- Mini-game assignments on non-anchor days shuffle each replay
